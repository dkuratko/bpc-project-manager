# Exploration brief — ESTIMATE PANE, ten FUNCTIONAL concepts
Round two. Round one produced ten layouts and they were all the same idea.

---

## What went wrong last time

Round one asked "how should these rows look." Every variant was a different
arrangement of the same list, so they all felt alike and none of them *did*
anything.

This round asks a different question: **what should this pane let you DO?**

Composing an estimate is not typing a document. It is deciding how to present a
job to a particular customer, deciding what to bundle together, deciding where
your margin sits, and writing the language that protects you. The document is
the *output* of those decisions. The pane should be a tool for making them.

Each concept below is a different answer. They are not skins — several imply
completely different interactions.

---

## Rules for all ten

```
WIDTH      ~800 px each, the pane's real width. identical, for comparison.
LANGUAGE   Windows 2000. Tahoma 11px. #c0c0c0, sunken white content, standard
           bevels. money right-aligned monospace with $ and two decimals.
DATA       the same job in every concept — see below.
LABEL      a black tag with the letter and its one-line promise.
SPACING    40 px of teal between concepts.
```

**The job, in all ten:** three lines — `INSTALLATION of Design #4471` $10,588.82
(subitems: Play structure $3,928.69, Posts and footings $6,660.13) ·
`Shade and site furnishings` $3,641.69 · `Site work` $3,269.49, overridden.
Cost $12,462.07, computed price $17,932.98, quoted $17,500.00.
Two add options: Footing installation $1,045.00 (on the shade line), Private
utility line locate $1,200.00 (job level). Three notes, one of them long:
*"footings not installed, to be installed before arrival by others, verified
level and within 1/4 in over 10 ft."*

## The ten

**[ A ] THE BUNDLER — grouping is the interface**
The pane's whole job is combining cost objects into lines. Objects appear as
draggable chips; lines are drop targets that total what lands in them. The
document is a by-product of how you bundled. Show one chip mid-drag.

**[ B ] MARGIN COCKPIT — you are pricing, not writing**
Every line carries `cost · price · margin %` and a small bar. Discount a line
and its margin cell goes red. The document text is secondary; the numbers are
the point. Show `Site work` at a visibly thinner margin than the other two.

**[ C ] PRESENTATION PRESETS — one control restructures everything**
A `Present as ▼` dropdown at the top: `Single lump sum · By design number · By
scope group · By trade · Fully itemised`. Choosing one rebuilds the whole
estimate instantly; you tweak from there. Show the dropdown open with `By scope
group` ticked, and the estimate as it stands under that choice.

**[ D ] LIVE PREVIEW SPLIT — watch the customer's page change**
Two halves. Left, the structure you edit. Right, the actual printed estimate
page as Grant Smith receives it, updating live. You never wonder what it will
look like.

**[ E ] TARGET SOLVER — name your number, it works backwards**
A field at the top: `I need to quote ▸ $16,500.00`. Beneath, the app shows the
implied effective markup, the shortfall against computed, and a ranked list of
where the money could come from — which lines have the most margin to give.

**[ F ] VERSION DIFF — what changed since A0**
The estimate shown against the version it was copied from. Added lines green,
removed struck through, changed amounts with their old value in grey. For a
customer who asks "what is different from the last one you sent."

**[ G ] MONEY MAP — see the shape of the job before the list**
A horizontal stacked bar across the top, one segment per line, width
proportional to money, labelled and priced. Click a segment to open that line
below. Visual first, detail second.

**[ H ] COMPOSE WIZARD — guided first pass**
For an empty estimate. Three steps across the top: `1 Structure · 2 Lines ·
3 Language`. Step 2 shown: your cost objects on the left, the lines you are
creating on the right, assigning as you go. Turns a blank page into a draft.

**[ I ] LINE INSPECTOR — one line at a time, properly**
A short list of lines at the top. Select one and the rest of the pane becomes
that line in full: exactly which cost objects it contains with a running
subtotal, its price and margin, and its notes in a **real multi-line edit box**
with a scrollbar. Assignment happens here.

**[ J ] PRINT-FIRST — you edit the actual page**
No editor chrome at all. The pane is the estimate as printed, on white, and you
type directly on it. A thin margin rail on the left shows cost and margin per
line, greyed, which never prints. What you see is exactly what they get.

## What to judge them on

```
1  does it help you DECIDE something, or only record something?
2  a 40-word exclusion that wraps
3  a job with nine lines
4  can you see which line you discounted, and what it cost you?
5  does it belong in the same application as the Scope tab?
```

Concepts may be combined later — a winner plus one good idea from another is a
fine outcome.
